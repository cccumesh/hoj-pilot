Part four

The launchpad

Jewellery is the first air. It is not the sky.

Chapter 12

Who deserves the first pilot

He did not ask which logo would look tallest on a slide. He asked which human situation would make Akxerai undeniable. A pilot that merely functions teaches the founder that the software runs. A pilot that changes a relationship teaches the market that the category is real. He wanted the second, and he was willing to be slower to get it.

The situation was a gift. One person knows why the object was chosen. Another person receives it away from the counter. If the object can carry the first person’s truth to the second without becoming a stunt, the company has a right to exist. Jewellery is full of that situation. So is a bottle of something kept for a night. So is a watch. So is a garment bought for a wedding. He chose jewellery first because the ticket is high, the trust is already a family business, and a failure of taste would be obvious. A category that punishes cheapness was the right teacher for a founder who had decided Akxerai would not feel cheap.

House of Jewels, Jalgaon, the Bafna family’s modern house on a road where many people sell gold, became the launchpad for a precise reason. Trust was already their tradition. A husband buying for his wife does not skip that kind of house only because the next shop has metal. Akxerai could add the sentence the next shop cannot copy by ordering the same designs: the gift still speaks, in that house’s world, when it is opened somewhere else. The four floors, the lounge, the fifty years, the young generation — all of that is the house’s. Akxerai does not become those floors. Akxerai powers the moment after the box leaves them.

Chapter 13

Sender and receiver

The magic, once he let himself say it plainly, was two seats. The sender scans the real card and talks. Not a form. Not “type a message for later.” A conversation in which a person actually says a name, a fear, a joke, a reason. That talk is kept on the code, as what was said, not as a novel the machine writes. The receiver, on a different phone, scans the same real object and meets the same presence. The presence is not a courier reading a card aloud. It is the character of that product’s life, carrying across what it knows, in its own fresh voice.

A third phone is the end of the magic, so the third phone is refused. A gift with an audience becomes content. Akxerai’s early product already knew this. The company that forgets it, in order to inflate a scan count, will win a metric and lose the reason brands trusted it. The dashboard may count the attempts. The product must not seat them.

This two-person door is the thing he will sell, in later years, to categories that are not jewellery. A bottle opened at a table of friends still has a buyer and a moment. A pair of shoes still has a person who chose and a person who wears. The seats remain two, not because every product is a wedding, but because a life that is open to anyone with a link is just a website. The link is the object. The object is in one place, in one hand, and then in the other.

Sender. Receiver. Nobody else. That rule is the product’s manners, and manners are what luxury actually is.
Chapter 14

The rooms that failed

Before the proof, there were rooms. Two, maybe three. He does not need their names in this book, because the lesson was not their signboard. In one room they wanted a QR that opened a page of collections. That is a brochure, and he had already refused to build a brochure. In another they wanted the trick, and they wanted it cheap, and they wanted it without the discipline of a real object. A cheap trick photographs well for a day and trains the customer to feel nothing. In another the conversation never got past curiosity. Curiosity is not a pilot. A pilot is boxes, codes, a person on each side, and a week in which something can go wrong.

He did not collect the nos as wounds. He implemented them. If a room asked for a page, the product’s first minute had to feel even less like a page. If a room asked for cheap, the price and the feeling had to be obviously the same decision. If a room would not put the card on a real box, it was not a customer yet. Failure, used this way, is a design input. Founders who hide the nos repeat them in the next city.

The failures also taught him the pitch he would not make again. He would not lead with the model’s name, or the word platform, or a promise of virality. He would lead with the dying story and the two people. Technology is the chapter for the team and the patent. It is not the sentence that opens a shop.

Chapter 15

Proof, and the decision not to become a jeweller’s feature

Then a room said yes in the only way that counts. Real cards. Real scans. A sender who talked as a person. A receiver who met the presence and felt that the gift had been waiting. People around the pilot were not polite. They were excited. That excitement is the proof that Chetan was not confused, and that Akxerai was not a private hobby with expensive words. A product that makes a small group of humans lean forward has earned the next step. A product that makes them nod has earned a rewrite.

He made one decision in the same week, and it is the decision that keeps this book from being a case study of a single shop. The launchpad would be loved, served, and made excellent. It would not be allowed to rename the company. House of Jewels powered by Akxerai is a true sentence. Akxerai as a department of one jeweller is a false one. The dashboard, the ledger, the verify, the voice, the two seats: those are the company’s. The character, the floors, the family trust: those are the house’s. Mixing them is how a founder becomes a vendor and stays there.

He also saw, in the way people held their phones, the content. A scan like this does not look like an advertisement. It looks like a moment. Moments travel. He did not hire a social team in the same week. He noticed that unique pictures of a real reaction were going to be part of distribution, and he refused to fake them. Virality, when it comes, has to be a side effect of a true pilot. Manufactured virality would make the third phone’s crime look normal.

Chapter 16

Excitement, and a second brand

The series moves from one proof to a second brand while the first is still being cared for. This is the step founders skip, and then they have an anecdote instead of a company. The second brand should be close enough that the product does not have to be reinvented, and different enough that a win cannot be dismissed as a family favour. Another jewellery house, or a house of watches and gifts in the same emotional weather, is the right second. A global sportswear logo is the wrong second. It flatters the founder and starves the system.

What the second brand tests is not Myra. Myra belongs to the first feeling of the launchpad. The second brand gets its own presence, its own short memory of the house, its own codes. What must transfer without a rewrite is the gate, the two seats, the ledger, the dashboard, the bill, and the manners. If those transfer, Akxerai is a product. If they do not, Akxerai was a project for one card.

Between the first excitement and the second signature, he runs the team pilot he owes himself. Not a new logo. His own people, even if “people” is still one founder and one trusted pair of hands. They try the photograph. They try the third phone. They leave and come back the next day. They read the dashboard. A brand should never be the first person to discover a broken lock. By the time the second house scans, the launchpad has already been insulted on purpose, and it held.

Part five

People

The best people are hired when the company can show them a living thing. That is how a founder keeps the company, and how the people get something worthy of them.

Chapter 17

Why the best people cost less equity later

In the empty months, a great engineer would have been right to ask for a co-founder’s share. There was no salary that meant anything, no user, no second phone lighting up. Risk was the whole offer. Chetan did not take that deal, because he did not yet have the product thinking fully stood up, and because a co-founder chosen in fear is a second founder of the fear.

After October 2026 the offer changes, and it should. There is a pilot-ready system. Then there is a launchpad with real scans. Then there is a second brand. Then there is a dashboard with talk, refusals, and returns. Data is not only a report. Data is a reduction in the other person’s risk. When risk falls, the equity required to attract a great person falls with it. Salary can rise. Equity stays precious. This is not stinginess. This is how the founder still owns the standard in 2031, and how early people who joined at the right time still own something that mattered.

Show the work, then hire. Hire the best, then pay them properly. Do not pay for loneliness with the cap table.

He will still give real equity to a few seats that take real risk: the first product engineer who will live in the system, the first brand lead who will sit in rooms and hear no, the first voice-and-character director who can make a second presence as alive as the first. Those three, if they come after the proof and not before it, are early. They are not half the company each. Everyone after them is hired as the company, with the pay of a serious company and the equity of a serious employee. By the time Akxerai is mid-size, the founder is competing for people with large firms. He wins some of them with the work, the standard, and a salary that does not insult them. He does not win them by pretending a four-year-old company is still a weekend hackathon.

Chapter 18

A company that hires “a team” hires a blur. Akxerai hires seats, in an order, each seat justified by a step the product has reached. This is the table he keeps. It is also the blueprint of a firm that can one day look like a large company without becoming a costume of one.

Chapter 19

A chance, in this company, is not luck. It is a team that matches the size of the room. Five of them mark the road. Each one fails if the previous one was faked.

One. The founder and a pilot-ready product, in an emotional category

No department. Jewellery. A launchpad house. The chance is that a sender and a receiver feel something, and that feeling is not a slide. This chance is available on the October page. It does not require a famous brand. It requires a real box and manners. If this chance is won, the company has a right to speak about the second.

Two. That same product, plus three early seats, in a second house

Brand lead, voice director, product engineer. The chance is repetition. A different presence, the same door. If the second house only works because the founder sleeps in their stock room, it is not a second proof. It is the first proof, tired.

Three. The bottle, returned to

The February idea comes back when verification is no longer fragile and a content lead exists. A bottle is a harder emotional sale than a jewel and a wider market. The chance is that the original sketch can wear the standard. A bottle brand that would have embarrassed the company in 2026 becomes a clean yes in the later year, because the product can refuse a photograph of a label and still feel alive at a table.

Four. A large national house

Now the room has lawyers and a brand team who have seen gimmicks. The chance exists only if the trust engineer, the finance lead, and the customer partner are already real. The national house is not impressed that a founder is passionate. It is impressed that the lock holds, the bill is predictable, and someone besides the founder will answer in the second year. Illustrations in the old notes — a large jeweller, a watch house, a premium bottle — are shapes of this room, not logos already signed. The book does not pretend signatures that were not made.

Team plus a product that is actually finished for that step: that is the only luck this company believes in.
Part six

The machine

One chapter for how it works, so the story never floats free of the system.

Chapter 20

How Akxerai works

A person opens a link. There is no app to install. The browser asks for the camera. The back camera is preferred, because the object is in the world, not in a mirror. The packaging is the target. On it, a three-dimensional presence anchors and can speak with a mouth that moves. The presence is the brand’s character. The system underneath is Akxerai, and the character is not supposed to talk about the system.

The gate

One live frame is judged in three layers. The first asks whether this is the right house or a readable code that belongs to the programme. The second asks whether the thing is physically here, or whether the camera is looking at a photograph, a screenshot, or another screen. The third asks whether the frame is good enough to trust. A pass needs all three and a real code. If several fail, a fake object outranks a missing brand, which outranks a bad frame. One failure locks the session. The presence says so. Retrying a lie in the same sitting is not a feature.

In the pilot the house is recognised as House of Jewels, as the Richera test card that maps to the code HOJ-RICHER, or as a printed code beginning with HOJ and a real suffix. The company later generalises that layer to whatever house is powered by Akxerai. The shape of the gate does not change when the logo does. That is what “powered by” means.

The two rows

Each code has a sender row and a receiver row, in the ledger this project actually uses, the table named ledger_threads. The first device to complete a true scan takes the sender seat. A different device takes the receiver seat. The same device, returning, keeps its seat and its memory. A third device finds the table full. Other tables, from other projects, are not this company’s memory and are not mixed into it.

Talk, memory, length

The buyer speaks. In the pilot, recognition of speech is the browser’s own. A heavier speech service sits in the project, switched off until it is wanted. Thought, for the verify, the opening, and the conversation, runs on a light model chain: Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, then 3.1 Flash-Lite. A short reply and a long reply do not promote themselves to a heavier model. They change how long the presence may speak. At the end of a session a heavier model, Gemini 3.6 Flash, writes the summary, once, and then the light chain if needed. The summary and a digest of what the person actually said are stored on that seat. The next visit continues. The presence is not supposed to recite the past like a transcript, and not supposed to invent a scene that was never spoken.

The intended voice is a cloned voice, reached through a server so the key is not in the page. If that voice is not configured, the browser can still speak, and the company treats that as a fallback, not as the product. A scan can be filmed. The first scan can note an age band, which only tunes manner. Production secrets stay on the server.

What a brand can see

A private dashboard reads the ledger. Scans, talk time by seat, whether both people came, praise in the customer’s own words, and the counters for a photo-fail, any other verify-fail, and a third phone. Talk and scans live on the thread. The three counters live in an insights field. Until that field exists on the live table, those three numbers stay quiet even while the lock in the room is already working. The company does not confuse a counter with a behaviour.

The flow, in one breath

Object in frame. Gate. If the gate fails, stop. If it passes, seat the device. Wake the presence. Talk. Speak. On leaving, write memory and usage against the code. On return, read memory. On a second human, open the other seat. On anyone else, refuse. On a picture of the object, refuse. That breath is the system. Every later feature — a harder gate, an owned voice, a new category — has to fit inside it. Features that need a third seat, or a pass for screenshots, or a memory that gossips beyond what was said, are not features. They are a different company, and this founder does not build that one.

Part seven

Money

The cost will fall. The price will not follow it down. That is the philosophy, and then the arithmetic.

Chapter 21

The price is a philosophy

A brand does not buy tokens. A brand buys the right to have its object behave as if the house is still in the room, with manners, memory, and a refusal to be faked. That right is worth more on the day Akxerai’s own machines speak cheaply than on the day the voice is rented. If the invoice falls just because the cost fell, the founder has told the market that Akxerai was a pass-through of someone else’s rate card. Pass-throughs do not become standards. They become line items, and line items get squeezed by the next clerk.

So the price is set by the feeling and by the category, and it has a floor. A jewel’s conversation is not priced like a can’s, but a can is not priced like a rounded-up electricity bill either. The floor is the company’s self-respect. Under it, Akxerai will not go, even if a volume brand asks, even if a spreadsheet shows that the GPU is almost idle. Idle capacity is not a reason to train a market to despise you. Idle capacity is a reason to sell the next house at the same dignity.

The launchpad’s first bill is allowed to be a teaching bill. Seventeen thousand boxes, an expectation of fifteen hundred real conversations at an average life, about one lakh nine thousand rupees in front of that house, using rented voice marked up into a rate the house can respect: voice placed at 27.50 dollars a million characters against a 25 dollar rack rate, thought placed at 0.36 and 3.00 dollars a million tokens against 0.30 and 2.50, rupees at 96 to the dollar. A full-use ceiling, if every box talked to the maximum planned, sits near twenty point seven lakh rupees, and is shown so the top is not a surprise. Quiet boxes are not punished. This bill is how a first house learns Akxerai. It is not how the standard is priced in 2030.

Never invoice your cost. Invoice the standard. Let the falling cost become payroll for better people, not a discount that makes you look small.
Chapter 22

The voice the company comes to own

Rented voice is the right instrument for the launchpad. It is fast, and the pilot cannot wait for a server room. It is the wrong instrument for a standard. The company’s own notes already did the grown-up sum. A graphics machine rented near fifty thousand rupees a month can speak on the order of two hundred million characters in that month. On that stack, a light conversation costs the company about two rupees, a half-hour about five, an hour about ten, Gemini included in that sketch. Ten serious brands and a few million scans sit near one and a half crore rupees of delivery a year, a few rupees each. The same volume on rented voice is a bill in the several crores, and at true scale it becomes a tax that eats the company.

He does not switch on the day the first house is happy. He switches when the second and third houses are renewing, when a trust engineer is in the building, and when a finance lead can watch a machine as a cost rather than as a dream. The first owned voice is one machine and one backup plan, serving a slice of traffic, compared honestly with the rented voice for sound and for failure. Brands are not told a science-fair story. They are told the voice remains the voice. The company’s margin changes in private, and the price on the invoice does not blink.

Do the scale sum without romance. Take a later year with twenty-five million engaged scans, averaging ten thousand characters of voice each. That is two hundred and fifty billion characters in the year. One machine, at two hundred million characters a month, covers two point four billion characters a year. The year needs on the order of one hundred and five such machines. At fifty thousand rupees a month, the rent is about six crore rupees. Add thought, and the company’s own blended delivery cost stays on the order of four to six rupees a scan, call it ten to fifteen crore rupees to deliver the year. That is the whole point of owning the voice. The bill to brands, if the philosophy holds, is not ten crore. It is the standard. The gap is where the best people are paid.

There is a discipline inside the machine count. Average use, not the ceiling, sizes the room. A ceiling of twenty-five thousand characters is how a single excited code is allowed to feel generous. A company that buys machines for the ceiling will build a cathedral for a crowd that comes on ordinary days. Size for the average. Keep a burst plan. Do not size the price for the average cost.

Now the picture that can carry a billion-dollar valuation without magic. It is a picture, with its assumptions visible, not a promise to a bank.

Twenty-five million engaged scans in a year. A blended price of one hundred and eighty rupees, because a jewel pays more and a bottle pays less, and neither pays four rupees. Usage revenue, four hundred and fifty crore rupees. Platform fees across something like two hundred houses, on the order of fifty crore, because being on the standard is itself a seat, not only a meter. Revenue on the order of five hundred crore rupees. At ninety-six rupees to the dollar, about fifty-two million dollars. Delivery, ten to fifteen crore. A company of around one hundred and eighty excellent people, fully loaded near forty lakh rupees each, is about seventy crore of people. The firm is still highly profitable. A market that believes this is the standard, and that the next year looks like more houses on the same door, can pay twenty times revenue. Twenty times fifty-two million is a billion dollars.

If the multiple is only twelve, the company is not a unicorn yet, and the book would rather say so than cheat the sum. Twelve times means the operating company is a remarkable business and the valuation is still a conversation. The road to the higher multiple is not a louder pitch. It is more categories on the same gate, renewals, owned voice, and a team that does not depend on one man’s travel. The billion is the market’s word for that proof. Chetan’s word for it, inside the building, remains simpler: do not be cheap, and do not be stuck on one house.

Part eight

The company

Small, then mid-size, then a standard, then a price. In that order, or not at all.

Chapter 24

Small

The small company is the three early seats plus the founder, then the trust engineer and the content lead, then a finance person who may be fractional until the second invoice hurts. The office, if it exists, is unimportant. The weekly rhythm is not. One day for the launchpad’s health. One day for the second brand. One day for the gate, because a gate that is not tended rots into a prompt. One day for the story the public is allowed to see, which is only true scans. One day for the founder to think about the category after next, so the company does not become its first customer.

Risk in this phase is concentration. One house can cough and the mood of the company coughs with it. The cure is the second brand, not a bigger slide about the first. Risk is also taste. A small team will imitate the first character unless a voice director has authority to refuse the imitation. Risk is cash, still. The launchpad bill is a teaching bill, not a payroll for fifty people. He does not hire the fiftieth person on the back of one lakh rupees and a dream. He hires the next seat when the step in the table is actually here.

What small must already feel, to the brands, is large in manners. Responses that are prompt. A lock that is explained without jargon. A bill that matches the philosophy. A presence that never sounds like a call centre. Companies die in this phase by looking scrappy in front of the customer and grandiose in front of the mirror. He reverses it. Scrappy in the internal tools if he must. Immaculate in the room.

Chapter 25

The patent learns to walk

The Pune provisional was a date and a description. The small company turns it into work. The trust engineer, with counsel, writes the gate as it actually behaves: three layers, the order of failures, the session lock, the two seats, the refusal of the third, the memory separated by role. Drawings a stranger can follow. No invented claim numbers in the marketing. No swagger that a provisional is a granted monopoly. A filing that matches the product, and a product that is allowed to grow only in ways the filing can be extended to meet.

The next features come in that spirit. Verification goes forward: better judgement of a screen, less mercy for a printed photograph, cleaner reading of a code, evidence kept long enough to know why a lock happened. The dashboard’s quiet counters become loud once the ledger can hold them, so the company can see abuse as a pattern and not as a story from one shop. None of this is a new company. It is the original invention growing teeth, which is what a provisional was for.

He does not let a feature roadmap be a list of wishes from the last meeting. A wish that breaks the two seats, or that lets a screenshot through because a campaign manager asked, does not enter the build. Counsel is there to protect the claim. The founder is there to protect the feeling. When those two disagree, they sit until the feature can satisfy both. That argument is a sign of health.

Chapter 26

Mid-size

Mid-size begins when a customer can be successful without the founder in the thread. The heads arrive: platform, studio, revenue, people. Platform guarantees that the hundredth house gets the same door as the second. Studio guarantees that the hundredth presence is not a mask on the first. Revenue guarantees that deals close at the floor and not under it. People guarantees that the best candidates are pursued with a process, not with midnight charm.

This is the phase where Akxerai starts to resemble the blueprint of a large firm, and where founders get lost. The lost ones either cling to every decision or vanish into the keynote. Chetan’s seat is written down so he cannot pretend he forgot it. He owns the standard: what “powered by Akxerai” is allowed to mean. He owns the next category: when the bottle, or the watch, or the house abroad, is actually ready. He owns the price philosophy when a large logo asks for a special humiliation. He does not own the deploy. He does not own the invoice format. He does not own the interview for a mid-level engineer. Letting those go is not retirement. It is the condition of a company that can reach 2031 without him as a single point of failure.

Mid-size is also where the early equity shows its wisdom. The first three seats, taken after the proof, are visibly owners of something. The fiftieth hire is well paid and proud, and does not expect the cap table of 2026. Culture is this distinction, said out loud, so nobody learns it as gossip.

Chapter 27

Becoming the standard

A standard is not a leaderboard. A standard is the default answer to a sentence. The sentence is: we want the product itself to carry the relationship, and we do not want to build a laboratory. When brand teams say “put it on Akxerai” the way they say “put it on the shelf,” the company has arrived at the thing the bottle month was reaching for, years early and far too small.

The signs are dull, which is how he trusts them. Renewals that do not require a new theatre. A second category live, then a third. A presence that the studio made without the founder in the script. A gate that counsel can draw and a shop can feel. A price floor that survived a famous buyer’s negotiation. An owned voice that customers did not notice as a migration, only as a company that still sounds alive. A hiring list aimed at people who could work anywhere, and a payroll that does not apologise.

Chapter 37

A price argument, heard in full

The buyer says: your cost is a few rupees, I have volume, I want a few rupees. It is the most intelligent attack the company will ever hear, because it is arithmetically true and philosophically fatal. Chetan’s answer is not a speech about brand love. It is a distinction. Cost is what it takes Akxerai to deliver a life once the voice is owned. Price is what it means to be the standard that life runs on. If those two numbers collapse into each other, the buyer has purchased a vendor of compute, and the next vendor of compute will win on a spreadsheet. Akxerai will not be that vendor.

He shows the quiet-code rule, which is the fairness inside the pride. Boxes that sleep are not taxed. The buyer is not paying for a catalogue’s vanity. The buyer is paying when a human actually enters. That is how a seventeen-thousand-box house can be shown an expected bill near one lakh nine thousand rupees and a frightening ceiling only if everyone talks to the maximum. Fairness is use. Dignity is the rate per use. Both can be true. A buyer who wants fairness without dignity wants a subsidy. A buyer who is offered dignity without fairness is being fleeced for unused paper. He refuses both errors.

Then the floor, by category, so the argument cannot hide in a blended average. A jewel can carry a high rate because the object already costs a month of someone’s salary and the memory is the point of the purchase. A bottle cannot carry a jewel’s rate, and it also cannot carry the GPU’s rate. Its floor is lower and still many times the cost, because the table, the gift, the refusal of a photograph, are the same door. Volume is allowed to change the package. Volume is not allowed to abolish the floor. A limited gift SKU at the floor is a yes. A whole catalogue at cost is a no he has already practised.

The owned-voice transition is where this argument is hardest, because the buyer may have heard that the company bought machines. He does not deny the machines. He denies the implication. The machines were bought so the company could hire better people and survive a scale that rented voice would have taxed to death. They were not bought so the invoice could be handed to the buyer’s procurement clerk as a discount. Procurement is welcome to audit use. Procurement is not welcome to audit his ambition and send him an invoice for it.

In the 2031 picture the sums stay visible so this argument never becomes mystical. Twenty-five million engaged scans. A blended one hundred and eighty rupees. About four hundred and fifty crore of usage, fees besides, on the order of five hundred crore together. Delivery in the low tens of crore once the voice is owned. People as the real cost, because the people are excellent. About fifty-two million dollars of revenue. Twenty times that is a billion dollars of valuation, if the market believes the door will still be the door next year. Twelve times is a great company that waits. He would rather wait than win the argument by lying about either number.

Chapter 38

What can still kill the company

The first killer is a yes that is too big and too early. A national logo in 2026, accepted on the logo’s terms, would have made him a subcontractor before he was a standard. The cure is the sequence. The fifth killer is the founder staying technically proud of being non-technical. Product thinking is necessary. It is not sufficient once the gate is under attack and the voice is a room of machines. The cure is the hiring table, obeyed. Specialists who are better than him. A seat he does not pretend to fill. The MBA he paused is not a wound in this story. It is a reminder that credentials are tools. He picks them up when the company needs the tool, including the credential of other people’s mastery.

The sixth killer is becoming the launchpad. If every deck, every hire, every feature is secretly for one house, the company ends when that house’s season ends. Love the house. Power the house. Keep a day for the bottle, the watch, the perfume, the room abroad. Scope is a calendar habit, not a poster.
